## Q1. Is each listed paper real, correctly attributed, and accepted where claimed? Current citation count?

### Takeaway
All 13 listed papers exist. Three venue entries need correcting. Adaptive Re-Ranking is peer reviewed at ICTIR 2026, not arXiv only. RetrievalRouter's EMNLP 2026 acceptance comes from the arXiv comment, not only the authors' page. LTRR was a SIGIR 2025 workshop paper, not a SIGIR main-track paper. The Chen paper has been retitled, LLM-Independent Adaptive RAG has a different first author than listed, and two "what it shows" claims need small fixes.

### Cited Findings

**Closest work**

- **RetrievalRouter: Joint Modality and Architecture Selection for Document Retrieval.** Emre Kuru, Mehmet Onur Keskin, Reza Farahbakhsh, Noel Crespi; arXiv:2608.25625, 26 Aug 2026, single version. The arXiv comment says "Accepted at EMNLP 2026". The EMNLP 2026 Anthology volume was not checked, so acceptance rests on the authors' own statement. S2: 0 citations. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25625)
  - The list says "routes among 7 pipelines". The paper evaluates 7 pipelines (BM25, TD, TL, TR, MD, ML, MR), but the router has only **5 arms** (BM25, TD, TR, MD, MR). TL and ML, the two late-interaction pipelines, are kept as static baselines only. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25625v1)
  - The "80,000+ queries with released per-query best-pipeline labels" claim is correct. They come from 11 datasets in REAL-MM-RAG, T2-RAGBench and MMDocRAG, mostly financial and scientific documents. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25625v1)

The list's short title is fine, but the full title shows it is a *post-hoc* (post-generation) method, so it fits RQ2 more than RQ1. The list says "only 40% of queries gained from a reranker", which inverts the figure: about 60% gain, about 40% do not. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25249v1)

## Q2. Which highly cited or top-venue papers on query routing / adaptive retrieval are missing?

### Takeaway
The biggest omissions are the canonical "when to retrieve" methods (Self-RAG, FLARE, DRAGIN, SeaKR) and the 2025 retriever-routing line (MoR at EMNLP 2025, RAGRouter at NeurIPS 2025). Self-Route (EMNLP 2024 industry track) is the standard cost-aware "RAG vs long-context" router. Most of these are top-venue and well cited, and Moskvoretskii et al. use several of them as baselines.

### Cited Findings
- **Self-RAG** (Asai et al., ICLR 2024). S2: **2,911**. The model emits reflection tokens that decide on demand whether to retrieve. It is the most-cited adaptive-retrieval method and a baseline in Moskvoretskii et al. **Suggest Ref in Section 4** (it is partly RQ2/agentic, but its retrieve-or-not token is a routing decision). — S2 batch (arXiv 2310.11511)
- **FLARE: Active Retrieval Augmented Generation** (Jiang et al., EMNLP 2023). S2: **929**. It retrieves when generated-token confidence is low. **Ref, Section 4.** — S2 batch (arXiv 2305.06983); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Active%20Retrieval%20Augmented%20Generation)
- **DRAGIN** (Su et al., ACL 2024). S2: 109. It decides when and what to retrieve from real-time information needs. **Ref, Section 4** (it is generation-time, not pre-retrieval). — S2 batch (arXiv 2403.10081)
- **SeaKR** (Yao et al., ACL 2025 per OpenAlex). OA: 27. Uses self-aware uncertainty to trigger retrieval. **Ref.** — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=SeaKR%20Self-aware%20Knowledge%20Retrieval%20for%20Adaptive%20Retrieval%20Augmented%20Generation)
- **RetrievalQA** (Zhang, Fang, Chen, ACL 2024 Findings per S2 venue tag). S2: 42. A benchmark for adaptive retrieve-or-not decisions. **Ref.** — S2 batch (arXiv 2402.16457)
- **SKR: Self-Knowledge Guided Retrieval Augmentation** (Wang et al., 2023; Findings of EMNLP 2023 from memory, unverified). OA: 62. An early query-level retrieve-or-not classifier. **Ref.** — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Self-Knowledge%20Guided%20Retrieval%20Augmentation%20for%20Large%20Language%20Models)
- **MoR: Better Handling Diverse Queries with a Mixture of Sparse, Dense, and Human Retrievers** (Kalra, Zhao, **To Eun Kim**, Cai, **Fernando Diaz**, Wu; EMNLP 2025 main, 2025.emnlp-main.601). S2: 14. A zero-shot, per-query weighted mixture of heterogeneous retrievers using pre- and post-retrieval signals. The mixture (about 0.8B parameters) beats each individual retriever by +10.8% and 7B retrievers by +3.9% NDCG@20. It is directly about per-query sparse/dense choice, which matches mmore's hybrid index, and it comes from the same group as LTRR. **Suggest Skim, Section 4.** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15862), [EMNLP 2025 listing](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2025.emnlp-main.601.bib)
- **RAGRouter: Learning to Route Queries to Multiple Retrieval-Augmented Language Models** (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2025 per S2/OA). S2: 13. It routes among retrieval-augmented LLMs, so the arms are model+retrieval combinations rather than retrievers. **Ref** (a top venue, but its arms are LLMs; name-clash with RAGRouter-Bench worth noting). — S2 batch (arXiv 2505.23052); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23052v2)
- **Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach (Self-Route)** (Z. Li et al., EMNLP 2024 Industry Track). S2: 196. The model routes each query to RAG or to long-context based on self-reflection, keeping long-context quality at much lower cost. This is the standard cost-aware per-query routing paper. **Ref, Section 4.** (The list "left out" the long-context debate. Self-Route is a routing paper, though, so one Ref line is justified.) — S2 batch (arXiv 2407.16833)
- **UniversalRAG** (Yeo, Kim, Jeong, Baek, Hwang; ACL 2026, arXiv 2504.20734). S2: 27. It routes each query to a modality- and granularity-specific corpus, from the query alone, and comes from the same KAIST group as Adaptive-RAG. **Suggest Skim, cross-listed RQ1/RQ3.** It is a top-venue, query-only router over corpora and modalities, very close to RetrievalRouter's idea. — S2 batch; [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.177/)

### Inferences
- If the list wants a clean "lineage" story for Section 4: Mallen 2023 / SKR 2023 (retrieve or not), then FLARE / Self-RAG / DRAGIN (when to retrieve during generation), then Adaptive-RAG 2024 (route by query complexity), then Moskvoretskii 2025 and Marina 2025 (simple baselines match), then LTRR / MoR / RouterRetriever 2025 (which retriever), then R³AG / Retriever Portfolios / RetrievalRouter 2026 (which retriever or pipeline, with oracle analysis).
- Self-RAG and FLARE do not need to be read for RQ1, but a thesis on adaptive RAG that does not cite them would look odd to examiners. Ref is enough.

## Q3. Which very recent (2026) papers overlap the thesis most and must be cited?

### Takeaway
Two papers must be added. **Retriever Portfolios** (ICML 2026, EPFL authors) is a principled best-of-k portfolio + router over retrievers. **One Size Does Not Fit All! (DRAG)** (Sep 2026) uses query performance prediction (QPP) to route per query among retrievers and generators. Both overlap RQ1 and the oracle experiment almost exactly. The SIGIR 2026 paper on QPP for query-variant selection is a strong Skim.

### Cited Findings
- **Retriever Portfolios: A Principled Approach to Adaptive RAG.** Miltiadis Stouras, Vincent Cohen-Addad, Silvio Lattanzi, **Ola Svensson** (EPFL / Google); arXiv:2605.31176, 29 May 2026; **"Accepted at ICML 2026"** per the arXiv comment. S2: 1 citation. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31176)
  - Method: choose a small, diverse portfolio of k retrievers from a large pool to maximise expected best-of-k over the query distribution. The objective admits near-optimal construction guarantees. A lightweight router then picks one retriever per query. It beats single-retriever and naive multi-retriever baselines on retrieval and answer quality, and has lower latency and token cost than inference-time hyperparameter tuning. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31176)
  - **Suggestion: Core, Closest work.** It formalises exactly the thesis's "virtual best vs single best" oracle gap as an optimisation objective, gives a way to prune the mmore configuration grid before training a router, is at a top venue, and is EPFL-local (Svensson is at EPFL IC), so the authors are easy to contact.
- **One Size Does Not Fit All! Dynamic Retriever and Generator Selection for RAG.** Neeraj Anand, Payel Santra, Partha Basuchowdhuri, Debasis Ganguly, Sumit Bhatia; arXiv:2609.17709, 15 Sep 2026; arXiv only, no venue listed. S2: 0 citations. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17709)
  - Findings: stronger retrieval yields larger gains than more generation effort, with diminishing, non-monotonic returns. DRAG_QPP is a training-free router that uses QPP for retriever choice and perplexity for generator choice, matching static baselines at lower latency. DRAG_SFT is a fine-tuned LLM router that beats static and training-free baselines. Tested on 3 LLM families and 4 QA benchmarks. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17709)
  - **Suggestion: Skim, Closest work or Section 4.** It bridges the "Earlier and adjacent work" QPP line and RQ1 routing, and Ganguly is a QPP specialist. It is arXiv only and 5 weeks old.
- **Can QPP Choose the Right Query Variant? Evaluating Query Variant Selection for RAG Pipelines.** Negar Arabzadeh, Andrew Drozdov, Michael Bendersky, Matei Zaharia; **SIGIR 2026** (Crossref container: Proceedings of the 49th International ACM SIGIR Conference, DOI 10.1145/3805712.3808571). S2: 2 citations. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22661), [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3805712.3808571)
  - Findings: there is a "utility gap": variants with the best nDCG often do not give the best generated answers. QPP reliably selects variants that beat the original query end to end, and cheap pre-retrieval predictors often match or beat post-retrieval ones. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22661)
  - **Suggestion: Skim, Section 4.** It is a top venue, Arabzadeh is the author of the list's "direct ancestor" CIKM 2021 paper, and the pre- vs post-retrieval predictor comparison is the thesis's RQ1-vs-RQ2 question. (Its arms are query variants, not pipelines; there may be overlap with the other researcher's QPP section.)
- **Lightweight Query Routing for Adaptive RAG: A Baseline Study on RAGRouter-Bench.** Prakhar Bansal, Shivangi Agarwal; arXiv:2604.03455, Apr 2026; arXiv only. S2: 3 citations. It trains 15 classifier × feature combinations (TF-IDF, MiniLM, structural features) to route queries on RAGRouter-Bench. A secondary blog reports TF-IDF+SVM at about 0.928 macro-F1 and 28.1% token savings, but that figure is **unverified** against the paper. **Suggestion: Ref** (a cheap-baseline recipe for the thesis's own router). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03455)
- **Most of the LLM Routing Gap Is Task Type.** Janghoon Lee; arXiv:2608.23023, Aug 2026; arXiv only. S2: 4 citations. It is about LLM routing and cites Chen's paper; the title claim is that much of the router-oracle gap is explained by task type. Full text not read. **Suggestion: Ref, next to Chen**, as a second router-vs-oracle analysis. It suggests a "route by query type" baseline for the oracle experiment. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23023)
- **Cost-Aware Query Routing in RAG: Empirical Analysis of Retrieval Depth Tradeoffs.** Sanjay Mishra (with Ganesh R. Naik in the journal version); arXiv:2606.02581, and an MDPI *AI* journal version (2026). S2: 2 citations. It routes per query among retrieval depths and generation profiles over FAISS dense retrieval. **Suggestion: do not add**, or Ref only: the venue is weak and its arms (top-k) overlap Adaptive-k and METIS already in the list. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02581), [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Cost-Aware%20Query%20Routing%20in%20RAG)
- Other 2026 items found but **not recommended** (arXiv only, 0 citations, or tangential): *SAGE: SLO-Aware Adaptive Retrieval for Production RAG Systems* (CoDIT 2026 per OpenAlex, a weak venue); *Policy Regret for Embedding Model Routing: Contextual Bandits with Low-Rank Experts* (Dai, Golrezaei, Jaillet; arXiv, theoretical); *Routing Is Least Learnable Where It Is Most Valuable* (web agents); *Route Me If You Can: A Benchmark for Query Reformulation Selection* (Le, Arabzadeh et al., arXiv). — [SAGE arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08237v1), [OpenAlex search results](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Policy%20Regret%20for%20Embedding%20Model%20Routing)

### Inferences
- With Retriever Portfolios (ICML 2026) and R³AG (ACL 2026), per-query retriever routing now has top-venue 2026 papers on public QA benchmarks. RetrievalRouter's own oracle-vs-router numbers (0.90 vs 0.755 nDCG@5) give the thesis a published reference point. If mmore's per-query oracle on humanitarian documents shows a similar or larger gap, that is directly comparable.
- Chen v3 and Lee 2026 both warn that the per-query oracle is inflated by noise and by coarse task-type effects. The thesis oracle experiment should (a) use deterministic retrieval metrics, or repeated generation runs if judging answers, and (b) report a "best config per query type" baseline next to "best single config" and "per-query oracle".

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of DRAG (2609.17709) or Retriever Portfolios, so I have no oracle numbers or datasets for them.
- I did not search the EMNLP 2026 accepted-paper list directly for other routing papers (not found online, or not checked), so there may be more concurrent EMNLP 2026 work.

## Q4. Which listed papers are weakest and could be dropped?

### Takeaway
No listed paper is fake. The weakest are recent arXiv-only, single-group or single-author items whose role overlaps a stronger paper. The candidates are: demote Evaluating Modern RAG to Ref; keep Chen only as a methodological Ref; possibly move Modality Relevance out of Closest work into RQ2/RQ3.

### Cited Findings
- **Evaluating Modern RAG (Kuru & Keskin):** arXiv only, 0 citations. Its pipeline set and authors overlap RetrievalRouter, which is at EMNLP 2026 and also reports static-pipeline trade-offs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23176), [RetrievalRouter HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25625v1)
- **Chen (routing oracle):** arXiv only, single author, about LLM routing on math/science QA, and the v3 comment says it supersedes its own earlier empirical claims. It has no confidence intervals. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03436)
- **Beyond the Query:** arXiv only, 0 citations. Its arms are RUN/SKIP rather than configurations, and the modalities are audio, video and documents. Its matched query-only control is still a useful methodological point for RQ1 vs RQ2. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12437)
- **Modality Relevance is not Modality Utility:** arXiv only, 2 citations. It is post-generation escalation on MultiModalQA, so it is closer to RQ2/RQ3 than to RQ1. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05438)
- **RAGRouter-Bench:** arXiv only, but S2 has 9 citations in about 9 months and it already has a follow-up baseline paper (2604.03455). Keep it as Skim. — S2 batch; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03455)

### Inferences
- Suggested priority changes:
  - Evaluating Modern RAG: Skim to **Ref**.
  - Chen: keep **Skim**, update the title, and drop the v1 "label noise" summary.
  - Beyond the Query: Core to **Skim** (arXiv only, binary decision, different modalities). Keep it in the list because its matched query-only control is the right experimental design for RQ1 vs RQ2.
  - Adaptive Re-Ranking: keep **Core** and change the venue to ICTIR 2026.
  - Add **Retriever Portfolios (Core)**, **MoR (Skim)**, **UniversalRAG (Skim)**, **QPP query-variant SIGIR 2026 (Skim)** and **DRAG (Skim)**.
  - Add **Self-RAG, FLARE, DRAGIN, Self-Route, RAGRouter (NeurIPS 2025)** as **Ref**.
- Mallen et al. could move from Skim to Ref. The thesis needs its finding (retrieval helps mostly for rare entities) and PopQA as a citation, but not a close read.

### Gaps
- "Weak" here is judged on venue, citations and topical fit. I did not assess the methodological quality of the arXiv-only papers from full text.
