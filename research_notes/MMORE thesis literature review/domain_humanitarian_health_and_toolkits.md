# Domain (humanitarian / global health / multilingual) and open-source RAG toolkits

Notes on method. Citation counts come from the Semantic Scholar Graph API batch endpoint (`/graph/v1/paper/batch`, fields title, venue, citationCount, externalIds), pulled on **2026-10-22**. They are live counts, not Google Scholar counts, and Google Scholar is usually higher. "S2 venue" means the venue string Semantic Scholar returns. Where I also confirmed the venue on ACL Anthology, a DOI, arXiv's Journal-ref/Comments field, or the publisher, I say "verified". Otherwise I write "venue per S2, not independently verified". Section suggestions use the user's list headings: Closest work; Earlier and adjacent; 1 Foundations; 2 Evaluation and benchmarks; 3 Surveys; 4 RQ1; 5 RQ2; 6 RQ3; 7 Agentic. ## Q0. MMORE's own metadata, and whether Feldman et al. 2026 is described accurately

### Takeaway
MMORE is an ICML 2025 CODEML workshop paper (non-archival workshop, posted to arXiv in Sep 2025) with only 3 S2 citations. The reading-list entry for Feldman et al. 2026 is accurate: 7,929 questions from UK Government public-health guidance, comparing dense, sparse and hybrid retrieval. It builds on a separate benchmark paper, PubHealthBench (Harris et al. 2025), which the list should also cite.

### Cited Findings
- **MMORE: Massive Multimodal Open RAG & Extraction.** Sallinen, Krsteski, Teiletche, Allard, Lecoeur, Zhang, Nemo, Kalajdzic, Meyer, Hartley. arXiv:2509.11937 (v1 15 Sep 2025, no later revisions). The arXiv Comments field says "originally submitted to the CODEML workshop for ICML 2025". There is no Journal-ref. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11937)
- The ICML 2025 virtual site lists it as a CODEML (Championing Open-source DEvelopment in ML) workshop poster, session 18 July 2025. — [icml.cc](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/48192)
- S2 citations: **3** (2026-10-22). Claims in the paper: a 3.8× speedup over single-node baselines; 40% higher accuracy than Docling on scanned PDFs; on PubMedQA, accuracy improves as retrieval depth grows. Code is at github.com/swiss-ai/mmore. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11937v1)
- **Healthier LLMs: Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Public Health Question Answering.** Feldman, Harris, Laurence, Loman, Higgins, Grayson, Soma, Pace-Bonello, Borowitz, Nonnenmacher. arXiv:2607.06641, 7 Jul 2026, no venue listed. The paper extends PubHealthBench (7,929 questions from UK Government public-health guidance) into a RAG setting. It compares dense, sparse and hybrid retrieval across several embedding models, corpus variants and chunk lengths. Hybrid retrieval consistently improves recall and ranking. Retrieved context lets small open-weight models match or beat larger models used without retrieval. An LLM judge with a rubric (faithfulness, completeness, clarity, factual consistency) is validated against two human annotators per item. S2 citations: 0. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06641)
- **Healthy LLMs? Benchmarking LLM Knowledge of UK Government Public Health Information** (PubHealthBench). Harris, Grayson, Feldman, Laurence, Nonnenmacher et al. arXiv:2505.06046, May 2025. More than 8,000 multiple-choice and free-form questions over 24 models. The best private models score above 90% on multiple choice, but no model scores above 75% on free form. The source guidance documents are released. S2 citations: 5. Venue per S2: arXiv. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06046v1)

### Inferences
- In the thesis, cite MMORE as "ICML 2025 CODEML workshop" (a workshop paper, not main conference). Do not use its citation count as a quality signal.
- Feldman et al. is the closest *domain* precedent, because it benchmarks retrieval configurations on official public-health guidance. It differs from this thesis in four ways: it is UK-only, English-only, uses born-digital gov.uk HTML/PDF rather than scanned multilingual WHO/ICRC/MSF documents, and has no per-query routing. Suggest **Closest work** (Core) for Feldman and **D** (Skim) for PubHealthBench.

### Gaps
- No OpenReview page for the CODEML submission was found, so I could not check whether the workshop was archival. Google Scholar counts were not checked (not reachable by script).

## Q1. Peer-reviewed or well-cited work on RAG over humanitarian, global-health and guideline documents

### Takeaway
I found **no peer-reviewed paper that does RAG over WHO, ICRC or MSF guidelines**. This supports the thesis's novelty claim. The nearest evidence is in four groups: (a) RAG over clinical or public-health guidelines in high-income settings (Ke et al. npj Digit Med 2025; Feldman 2026); (b) humanitarian NLP datasets and LLM pipelines (HumSet, EMNLP Findings 2022; LLM situation reporting, Sci Rep per S2; DisastQA, ACL 2026 per S2); (c) LLM clinical decision support trials in African primary care (Nature Medicine 2026; Nature Health 2026); (d) Meditron and AfriMed-QA for medical LLMs in low-resource settings.

### Cited Findings
Guideline RAG and medical RAG beyond MIRAGE:

The system does RAG over preoperative clinical guidelines with 10 LLMs. S2 citations: **157**. Suggest **D**, Skim. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01519-z.pdf)
- **Almanac: Retrieval-Augmented Language Models for Clinical Medicine.** Zakka, Chaurasia, Shad, Dalal, Kim et al. arXiv:2303.01229. S2 lists only the Research Square preprint version. From memory, it was published in *NEJM AI* in 2024, which I have **not verified** here. S2 citations: **301** (2026-10-22). It is an early clinical RAG system grounded in curated sources. Suggest **D**, Ref. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01229)
- **Clinfo.ai: An Open-Source Retrieval-Augmented LLM System for Answering Medical Questions using Scientific Literature.** Lozano, Fleming, Chiang, Shah. PSB 2024 (venue per S2). S2 citations: 74. It is an open-source medical RAG system with its own evaluation set (PubMedRS-200). Suggest **D**, Ref. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16146)
- **Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Medicine with Iterative Follow-up Questions** (i-MedRAG). Xiong, Jin, Wang, Zhang, Lu et al. Pacific Symposium on Biocomputing 2025 (verified by DOI 10.1142/9789819807024_0015). S2 citations: **143**. It is the MedRAG authors' follow-up, adding iterative query reformulation. - **Comprehensive and Practical Evaluation of RAG Systems for Medical QA** (MedRGB). Ngo, Nguyen, Dernoncourt, Nguyen. arXiv:2411.09213, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: 18. It tests medical RAG robustness to noise and misinformation. Suggest **2 Evaluation**, Ref.
- **Towards Omni-RAG: Comprehensive RAG for LLMs in Medical Applications** (MedOmniKB). Chen, Liao, Jiang, Wang, Guo et al. ACL 2025 (venue per S2; main vs Findings not verified). S2 citations: 25. It routes queries to multiple heterogeneous medical knowledge sources, which is close to the thesis's routing idea. Suggest **4 RQ1**, Skim. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02460)
- **Benchmarking Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Medicine** (MIRAGE/MedRAG; already in the list). It appears in the ACL Anthology as *Findings of ACL 2024* (2024.findings-acl.372). S2 labels it "ACL", which is imprecise. S2 citations: **675**. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.372.pdf)

Surveys of medical RAG (useful for the "low-resource is under-studied" claim):
- **Improving LLM applications in biomedicine with RAG: a systematic review, meta-analysis, and clinical development guidelines.** Liu, McCoy, Wright. *JAMIA* 2025 (verified by DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocaf008). S2 citations: **212**. Suggest **3 Surveys**, Skim.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Medicine: A Scoping Review of Technical Implementations, Clinical Applications, and Ethical Considerations.** Yang, Wong, Li, Li, Zhu et al. arXiv:2511.05901. Humanitarian and crisis NLP:
- **HumSet: Dataset of Multilingual Information Extraction and Classification for Humanitarian Crisis Response.** Fekih, Tamagnone, Minixhofer, Shrestha, Contla et al. EMNLP 2022 (S2 says EMNLP; I believe it is Findings of EMNLP 2022, **not verified**). S2 citations: 9. It contains humanitarian response documents annotated by experts in English, French and Spanish. S2 citations: 2. The pipeline clusters documents and then runs RAG question answering over more than 1,100 ReliefWeb and other verified documents covering 13 humanitarian events. It reports 86.3% answer relevance, and citation precision and recall both above 76%. It is the closest humanitarian RAG paper. Suggest **D**, Core. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19475)

LLMs for global health and low-resource settings:

- **AfriMed-QA: A Pan-African, Multi-Specialty, Medical Question-Answering Benchmark Dataset.** Olatunji, Nimo, Owodunni, Abdullahi, Ayodele et al. **ACL 2025 (long)**, verified by DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.96. S2 citations: 49. Suggest **D**, Skim (closed-book QA, not RAG).
- **Generative AI-enabled clinical decision support system in primary care: a pragmatic, cluster-randomized trial.** Agweyu, Mwaniki, Menon, Korom, Isaaka et al. *Nature Medicine* 2026 (verified by DOI 10.1038/s41591-026-04503-6). - **AI-based Clinical Decision Support for Primary Care: A Real-World Study** (Penda Health "AI Consult"). Korom, Kiptinness, Adan, Said, Ithuli et al. arXiv:2507.16947, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: 43. Suggest **D**, Ref.
- **HealthBench: Evaluating LLMs Towards Improved Human Health.** Arora, Wei, Hicks et al. (OpenAI). arXiv:2505.08775, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: **510**. It includes global-health and multilingual themes, but uses closed-book rubric grading. Suggest **2 Evaluation**, Ref.

### Inferences
- Peer-reviewed work on *retrieval over humanitarian or global-health guideline documents* is nearly absent. The nearest pieces are the UK public-health RAG work (Feldman 2026; arXiv), single-specialty guideline RAG (Ke 2025, npj Digit Med), and humanitarian situation-report RAG (Decostanzi et al.). Together with the scoping-review figure that only 6 of 251 medical RAG studies were done in low-resource settings, this gives a sourced gap statement.
- **A small new section D, "Domain: humanitarian and global health", is justified.** Suggested contents (about 8 papers): Feldman 2026 (or keep it in Closest), PubHealthBench, Ke 2025, Decostanzi 2025, HumSet, Yang 2025 scoping review, Meditron-70B, AfriMed-QA, the Kenyan Nature Medicine RCT, and MedExpQA/MMedBench (see Q2).
- Kenya and Malawi studies show that guideline alignment (local protocols) is the evaluation axis global-health deployments care about. This supports using guideline-grounded faithfulness metrics rather than MCQA accuracy alone. This is my inference.

### Gaps
- I found no paper on LLM or RAG tools built *by* ICRC, MSF, or WHO, or evaluated on their guidelines. Searches for "WHO guidelines RAG" returned only GitHub demos and non-peer-reviewed items.
- The Meditron 3 / Llama-3-Meditron paper: a Meditron 3 description is hosted on upcommons.upc.edu, but I could not confirm its authors, venue or citations. The MOOVE evaluation results are not yet published as far as I found.
- Almanac's NEJM AI venue and HumSet's Findings status are from memory and should be confirmed on the publisher page and the ACL Anthology.

## Q2. Multilingual and French medical QA and retrieval benchmarks usable for non-English evaluation

### Takeaway
No French medical *RAG-over-guidelines* benchmark exists. The usable pieces are: MedExpQA (EN/ES/FR/IT medical QA with RAG baselines; AI in Medicine 2024); FrenchMedMCQA and DrBenchmark (French medical; LOUHI 2022 and LREC-COLING 2024); MMedBench (Nature Communications 2024; includes French); WorldMedQA-V (NAACL 2025 per S2); MIRACL (TACL 2023; includes French) for general retrieval; and Chirkova et al. 2024 and MIRAGE-Bench for multilingual RAG methodology. MMed-Bench-IR (arXiv, Jun 2026) is the only multilingual *medical retrieval* benchmark that includes French that I found.

### Cited Findings
- **MedExpQA: Multilingual Benchmarking of LLMs for Medical QA.** Alonso, Oronoz, Agerri. *Artificial Intelligence in Medicine* 2024 (verified by DOI 10.1016/j.artmed.2024.102938). S2 citations: **106**. It covers English, Spanish, French and Italian, tests RAG against gold explanations, and finds accuracy markedly lower for non-English languages. Suggest **D**, Skim. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0933365724001805)
- **FrenchMedMCQA: A French Multiple-Choice QA Dataset for Medical Domain.** Labrak, Bazoge, Dufour, Daille, Gourraud et al. LOUHI workshop 2022 (venue per S2). S2 citations: 50. Suggest **D**, Ref. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04280)
- **DrBenchmark: A Large Language Understanding Evaluation Benchmark for French Biomedical Domain.** Labrak, Bazoge, El Khettari, Rouvier et al. LREC-COLING 2024 (venue per S2). S2 citations: 12. Suggest Ref.
- **DrBERT: A Robust Pre-trained Model in French for Biomedical and Clinical domains.** Labrak et al. S2 lists only bioRxiv. I believe it is ACL 2023, **not verified**. S2 citations: 72. Use it as a candidate French biomedical encoder. Ref.
- **Towards building multilingual language model for medicine** (MMedC / MMedBench / MMedLM). Qiu, Wu, Zhang, Lin, Wang et al. *Nature Communications* 2024 (verified by DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-52417-z). S2 citations: **247**. It covers six languages including French. Suggest **D**, Skim.
- **Apollo: A Lightweight Multilingual Medical LLM towards Democratizing Medical AI to 6B People** (XMedBench). Wang, Chen, Chen, Wang, Zhen et al. arXiv:2403.03640, venue per S2: none. S2 citations: 61. Includes French. Ref.
- **WorldMedQA-V: a multilingual, multimodal medical examination dataset.** Matos, Chen, Placino, Li, Pardo et al. NAACL 2025 (per S2; main vs Findings not verified). S2 citations: 18. Ref.
- **MMed-Bench-IR: A Heterogeneous Benchmark for Multilingual Medical Information Retrieval.** Lee, Jang, Goh, Choi. arXiv:2606.24200, Jun 2026. S2 citations: 0. It covers six languages including French and includes a multilingual RAG evidence-retrieval task with 2,040 queries. The French translation-quality score is lower (0.86) than Spanish (1.00). Suggest **D** or **2 Evaluation**, Skim (new, not peer-reviewed). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24200)
- **MIRACL: A Multilingual Retrieval Dataset Covering 18 Diverse Languages.** Zhang, Thakur, Ogundepo, Kamalloo, Alfonso-Hermelo et al. *TACL* vol. 11, 2023, pp. 1114–1131, verified on ACL Anthology (2023.tacl-1.63, DOI 10.1162/tacl_a_00595). S2 citations: **239** (counted on the TACL DOI record; the separate arXiv record shows 76, so S2 splits the counts). It contains 78k queries and 726k native-speaker relevance judgments, including French. Suggest **2 Evaluation**, Ref. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.tacl-1.63)
- **Retrieval-augmented generation in multilingual settings.** Chirkova, Rau, Déjean, Formal, Clinchant et al. (NAVER Labs Europe, the BERGEN team). KnowLLM workshop at ACL 2024 (ACL Anthology 2024.knowllm-1.15). S2 citations: 57. Built with BERGEN, it covers 13 languages and finds French near the top of the non-English group. It gives practical guidance on multilingual retrievers and prompting. Suggest **D** or **1 Foundations**, Skim. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.knowllm-1.15.pdf)
- **MIRAGE-Bench: Automatic Multilingual Benchmark Arena for RAG Systems.** Thakur, Kazi, Luo, Lin, Ahmad. NAACL 2025 (per S2). S2 citations: 25. Covers 18 languages including French. Note: this is a different benchmark from medical MIRAGE. Ref.
- **M3-Embedding (BGE-M3).** Chen, Xiao, Zhang, Luo, Lian et al. *Findings of ACL 2024* (verified by DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.137). S2 citations: **2,053**. It provides dense, sparse and multi-vector retrieval from one multilingual model, which makes it a natural candidate retriever for French documents. Suggest **1 Foundations** or **6 RQ3**, Ref.
- **MTEB-French.** Ciancone, Kerboua, Schaeffer, Siblini. arXiv:2405.20468, venue per S2: none. S2 citations: 7. Use it to choose a French embedding model. Ref.

### Inferences
- For French evaluation, the most defensible plan is to build a small in-domain test set from French WHO, ICRC or MSF documents. It can be validated against MedExpQA (French medical QA with RAG) and the MIRACL French subset (general retrieval sanity check), with Chirkova et al. 2024 as the method reference for multilingual RAG pitfalls such as answer-language drift and retriever choice.
- BGE-M3 is worth citing because it can produce all three retrieval types the thesis routes between (sparse, dense, multi-vector) from one multilingual model.

### Gaps
- I found no benchmark of question–passage pairs over French public-health or humanitarian guidelines. MedExpQA's French part is translated medical-exam data, not guideline documents. This gap is itself a possible contribution.

## Q3. Open-source RAG and document-ingestion toolkits with papers, to position MMORE against

### Takeaway
Toolkits with research papers fall into two groups. **RAG research frameworks**: FlashRAG (WWW 2025 resource, 256 S2 citations), BERGEN (EMNLP 2024, 48), RAGLAB (EMNLP 2024 per S2), AutoRAG (arXiv), FlexRAG (ACL 2025 per S2), and RAG-Anything (arXiv, multimodal). **Document ingestion and parsing**: Docling (technical report and AAAI 2025 workshop paper), MinerU (358), Nougat (ICLR 2024), olmOCR, and OmniDocBench as the parsing benchmark (CVPR 2025). None of them combines distributed multimodal ingestion with hybrid RAG the way MMORE does. Several, such as FlashRAG, BERGEN and AutoRAG, are stronger on systematic configuration benchmarking, which is the thesis's RQ3 territory. RAGFlow, Haystack, LlamaIndex and LangChain have **no research papers**, so cite them as software (GitHub URL plus version).

### Cited Findings
RAG frameworks:
- **FlashRAG: A Modular Toolkit for Efficient Retrieval-Augmented Generation Research.** Jin, Zhu, Dou, Dong, Yang et al. WWW 2025 Companion (resource paper; DOI 10.1145/3701716.3715313). S2 citations: **256**. It ships 36 benchmark datasets and reimplements many RAG methods. It is the main reference point for "benchmarking RAG configurations in one toolkit". Suggest **6 RQ3** or a toolkit subsection, Skim. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13576)
- **BERGEN: A Benchmarking Library for Retrieval-Augmented Generation.** Rau, Déjean, Chirkova, Formal, Wang et al. (NAVER Labs Europe). S2: EMNLP 2024. I believe it is *Findings of EMNLP 2024*, **not verified on the Anthology**. S2 citations: 48. It argues for reproducible RAG benchmarking and was used for the multilingual RAG study above. Suggest **2 Evaluation**, Skim. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01102)
- **RAGLAB: A Modular and Research-Oriented Unified Framework for RAG.** Zhang, Song, Wang, Tang, Li et al. EMNLP 2024 per S2 (probably the System Demonstrations track, not verified). S2 citations: 15. Ref.
- **AutoRAG: Automated Framework for optimization of Retrieval Augmented Generation Pipeline.** Kim, Kim, Han, Eibich. arXiv:2410.20878, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: 50. It runs an AutoML-style search over RAG modules per dataset. This is the static "best pipeline per corpus" baseline that per-query routing improves on. Suggest **6 RQ3**, Skim.
- **FlexRAG: A Flexible and Comprehensive Framework for RAG.** Zhang, Feng, Zhang. ACL 2025 per S2 (track not verified). S2 citations: 3. Ref.
- **RAG-Anything: All-in-One RAG Framework.** Guo, Ren, Xu, Zhang, Huang (HKU, the LightRAG authors). arXiv:2510.12323, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: 30. It is an open-source *multimodal* document RAG framework (text, images, tables, equations), which makes it the closest functional competitor to MMORE. Suggest a toolkit subsection or **Closest work: positioning**, Skim.

Document ingestion and parsing:
- **Docling Technical Report.** Auer, Lysak, Nassar, Dolfi, Livathinos et al. (IBM). arXiv:2408.09869, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: 59. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09869)
- **Docling: An Efficient Open-Source Toolkit for AI-driven Document Conversion.** Livathinos, Auer, Lysak, Nassar, Dolfi et al. arXiv:2501.17887. The arXiv record says it was accepted at the *AAAI 2025 Workshop on Open-Source AI for Mainstream Use* (workshop, not main proceedings). S2 citations: 89. MMORE benchmarks itself directly against Docling. Suggest **Closest work: positioning**, Core (cite either version; the AAAI-workshop version is the more formal one). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17887)
- **MinerU: An Open-Source Solution for Precise Document Content Extraction.** Wang, Xu, Zhao, Ouyang, Wu et al. (Shanghai AI Lab). arXiv:2409.18839, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: **358**. Ref.
- **Nougat: Neural Optical Understanding for Academic Documents.** Blecher, Cucurull, Scialom, Stojnić (Meta). ICLR 2024 (per S2). S2 citations: 340. Ref.
- **olmOCR: Unlocking Trillions of Tokens in PDFs with Vision Language Models.** Poznanski, Borchardt, Dunkelberger, Huff, Lin et al. (AI2). arXiv:2502.18443, venue per S2: arXiv. S2 citations: 138. It is the VLM-based OCR alternative relevant to scanned humanitarian PDFs. Ref.
- **OmniDocBench: Benchmarking Diverse PDF Document Parsing with Comprehensive Annotations.** Ouyang, Qu, Zhou, Zhu, Zhang et al. CVPR 2025 (verified by DOI 10.1109/CVPR52734.2025.02313). S2 citations: 192. It is the standard benchmark for comparing parsers such as MMORE, Docling, MinerU and olmOCR. Suggest **2 Evaluation**, Skim.
- No research paper was found or expected for RAGFlow (infiniflow), Haystack (deepset), LlamaIndex or LangChain. They are software projects, to be cited by repository and version. (This is from my own knowledge; I did not search exhaustively for workshop papers.)

### Inferences
- A positioning table in the thesis could compare MMORE, Docling, MinerU, olmOCR (ingestion), FlashRAG, BERGEN, AutoRAG (RAG benchmarking) and RAG-Anything (multimodal RAG), across these axes: input modalities, scanned-PDF OCR, distributed processing, hybrid retrieval, configuration benchmarking, per-query routing, and peer-reviewed status. As far as the abstracts show, none offers *per-query* routing, so that remains the thesis's methodological hook within the toolkit landscape.
- FlashRAG and BERGEN are the strongest peer-reviewed comparators, by venue and citations, for "a toolkit used to benchmark RAG configurations". Cite both in RQ3.
- Because MMORE's main empirical claim is better scanned-PDF accuracy than Docling, OmniDocBench is the natural external benchmark to re-check that claim, if ingestion quality enters the thesis.

### Gaps
- Not verified: BERGEN as Findings vs main EMNLP 2024, RAGLAB's and FlexRAG's tracks (likely demo), WorldMedQA-V and Omni-RAG main vs Findings, and the Scientific Reports and Cell Reports Medicine venues of two 2025 papers. Check each on the ACL Anthology or publisher page before quoting.
- Google Scholar counts were not collected. All counts are Semantic Scholar counts from 2026-10-22, which are typically lower than Google Scholar's.
- Out of scope and not searched, as instructed: chunking, GraphRAG, retriever training, long-context vs RAG.
